# Capacities on YouTube (@CapacitiesHQ): what they say, how they show it, and what memrynote should take

Snapshot: 2026-10-16. Channel: 12.3k subscribers, 47 long-form videos + 30 Shorts with metadata (the channel lists also hold 1 long-form, "Why is it Helpful to Tag and Link?", ~3.1k views, and 2 Shorts without metadata; they are left out of the medians).

Evidence used: `analyze_meta.txt`, `stats.txt`, `meta/*.json` (all 77), transcripts of 10 long-form benchmark videos, the brand film and 2 Shorts (all read in full; `8s5oF8fBYS0` has no captions), all 27 storyboard contact sheets plus denser opening/ending sheets I built from the raw storyboards (`work/sheets/*_openend.jpg`), all 4 thumbnail sheets plus 3 full-size thumbnails, the top 20 comments on 3 videos (`comments/*.json`), and our film (`ours_sheet.jpg`, `narration.json`, `out/take/timeline.json`, `scenes.mjs`, phone-size renders in `work/sheets/ours_phone*.jpg`). Working notes: `work/visual_notes.md`, `work/thumb_notes.md`.

## 0. Caveats that change how to read the numbers

- **Paid reach.** September and October 2026 look like an ad push. The brand film (2026-09-09, 4K, empty description), "Getting Started" (2026-10-05), "Tasks, Projects and Progress Tracking..." (2026-09-07), "The 5 Core Habits..." (2026-10-08) and the 2026-09-22 release earn 200-613 views/day. Earlier videos of the same formats earn 10-30 views/day. All five sit at 1.7-2.5% likes (channel median 3.23%). Four of them have only 0.4-1.5 comments per 1k views (channel median 4.3); the release is the exception at 4.4 comments/1k, which suggests it is partly organic. Conclusions that rest on these videos are marked **[PAID?]**.
- **Like ratio is confounded by audience.** Release and launch videos are watched mostly by existing users, who like and comment more (4.2% likes, 5.5 comments/1k). Tutorials found through search reach prospects, who do neither as much (2.2%, 2.1/1k). So a below-median like ratio on an old tutorial is weak evidence of ads. I flag a video only when high views, low likes and low comments coincide.
- **No retention data.** Heatmaps are missing for 0/47 videos, so likes, comments/1k and views/day stand in for retention.
- **Storyboard tiles are 320x180 px.** That is about the size of the video in a portrait phone player (~390 pt wide), so "unreadable in the tile" means unreadable for a portrait phone viewer. The tiles do not show cursor size reliably or YouTube end-screen overlays, which the player draws on top of the video.
- **Captions** are YouTube's (27/47 videos have manual English subs), so small word errors appear in quotes ("Reka"/"Lewis" for Réka/Luis).

Channel baseline for long-form: median 4,183 views, 3.23% likes, 4.3 comments/1k.

**Cadence.** In the last 12 months: 31 long-form (≈2.6/month) and 30 Shorts. The Shorts came in a burst: started 2026-03-19, peaked at 9 in May and 6 in June, then fell to 2-4 a month. Release videos follow the product's release train: 12 releases between 2026-01-26 and 2026-09-22, median 17 days apart (releases 65 and 69 got no video). Methodology videos come about monthly.

**What performs best organically:**

1. **A launch of a feature people had been waiting for.** "Introducing Task Management in Capacities!" has 19,901 views and 116 comments (5.8/1k, above the channel median). Its 2.3% like ratio is low, but the comments are clearly organic. They come from existing users ("I've been counting the days to see this new up-date", "This absolutely convinced me to upgrade to pro!") and from new ones ("New user here... found Capacities (by pure chance)"). It is the channel's #1 video by a wide margin.
2. **Methodology videos that name a known framework or a universal problem.** At normal like ratios, GTD (5,256 views, 3.1%, 48 views/day) and PARA (3,164, 3.4%, 37/day) earn about twice the daily views of a typical 2026 release (17-21/day; only Mobile 2.0 reached 68/day). "How to Capture, Review, and Use Your Notes" (16,458 views, 2.8%, 44 comments) never names Capacities in its title. Its comments are full of switchers ("I am a recovering Obsidian user...", "Switchting from notion as we speak", "Let me download this app!"). It is the channel's acquisition video.
3. **Releases and single-feature launches win on engagement, not reach.** 4.2% likes and 5.5 comments/1k, with a ceiling around 3-6k views. analyze_meta's "best organic" list (like ratio ≥ median, ranked by views) is entirely launches, releases, team chats and PARA, topped by "Two-Way Linked Properties" at 6,006. That is a realistic organic ceiling for a 12k-subscriber tool channel.
4. **Shorts are the weakest format.** Median 778 views, 1.9% likes, zero comments; the best has 2,230 views (see §5).
5. **The brand film's 12.7k views and Getting Started's 517 views/day are paid-reach numbers [PAID?],** not evidence about the formats.

### 2.3 How sections are introduced and chained

- **One chapter is one step or one feature,** announced with a short transition. "Now, there's a few buttons to explore here, but we'll start with the one on the left." (Task Management 1:35). "We also have some tabs related to scheduling. So let's talk about that next." (5:18). "Which leads me into the next thing which you may have noticed, and that is this reviewed checkbox." (New Release 1:38). "First things first..." / "next up is something that has been long awaited..." / "On top of this we've also added..." / "And the final feature that we have added is..." (Release 70). Counts across 18.2k words: "So" 263× (14 per 1k words), "Now" 105×, "Okay, next up" in releases.
- **The running example is the table of contents.** In Getting Started the daily note holds four captured items. The video walks them in order: "I'll start with these two things at the bottom because they're easy. They are simply tasks" (0:57); "Now the other two are a bit different. They're not just individual tasks. They're really a bigger project." (1:33); "Now on to the next bullet point." (4:41). The demo data carries the agenda.
- **Open loops keep people watching:** "You'll see why that's useful later." (Getting Started ~3:16); "It will also create this task tab... and we will come back to that later." (Task Management 0:31); "you'll see the answer for why that is in the next video" (Organize ~3:15).
- **The problem comes before the solution:** "Well, before I show you that, I have to show you the problem." (Tasks/Projects ~1:20). Two-Way Linked Properties shows the manual @-link and backlink first (0:00-1:40), then the new setting.
- **Rules of thumb make the abstract decidable:** "can this thing be done in just one sitting? Can you think of a task and complete it within about 30 to 60 minutes? If you can, you don't need a project." (Tasks/Projects ~3:24). "a general rule of thumb with these views is that if you're not seeing what you expect to see, it's because you haven't filled in a property." (Getting Started ~7:52). "We're looking for two signals" (Capture 0:16).

### 2.4 Why before how? Running persona?

- **Launches, workflows and methodology: yes.** Why first, then how, often inside each section too: "So when I come into my calendar and I see the task I have to get done, I don't have the cognitive load of working out what I need to do right now" (Task Management ~6:26).
- **Releases** give what, how, who gets it, and limits, with a user-feedback why: "We were hearing that it was providing friction to have blocked in this in progress group" (New Release ~13:32); "we were hearing from people using tools other than Microsoft and Google" (Release 70 3:23).
- **The persona is the presenter herself,** and the demo data is the team's own work. Project "Task Management Launch" with the task "Film Video" ("for me, filming the video is the ultimate priority", ~6:26). Project "Release 70" (Tasks/Projects). A colleague "Réka" with her real photo (Task Management 10:35). An "Email Campaign for latest Pro Features" with Réka (Getting Started). Life sits next to work: "Grandma's 80th Birthday Party" with cake photos (Getting Started 5:25), "Find a nice cheese set to buy", "box of tissues" (5 Habits 2:11). Concept videos use culture: Jane Austen, Brian May and Taylor Swift, the Natural History Museum. "Capture-Review-Use" uses her real notes: "I've put some real examples of previous Daily Notes of mine into today's Daily Note." (1:23).

### 2.5 Recaps

- Methodology and orientation videos close with a spoken recap of the framework. "So back in the Daily Notes to close off this video, I hope that this has given you an insight into a foundational workflow that you can use where you capture what matters, you review it regularly, you link ideas together..." (Capture 12:17). "So, those are the five habits. We had capture, clarify, organize, act, and reflect." (5 Habits 8:08). "So that's the core difference. Collections drill down into an object type, and tags sort of go the other way..." (Organize 9:06). "So this is contextualized task management in capacities." (Task Management ~10:47).
- **Bookends:** "Now, we're going to end the video back in the calendar to see how it has changed since the start of the video." (Getting Started ~8:21). The Capture video also ends where it began, on the daily note.
- Releases have no recap: "So that is everything for this release." (Release 70 4:56).

### 2.6 CTAs

- **End of video only, always a question prompt:** "If you have any questions about this, please let me know in the comments below." Five of 10 transcripts say "comments below"; 7 of 10 say "let me/us know". Variants invite participation: "Or if you would like to share an idea for how to use it, please do. We would love to know" (Two-Way); "If you have any questions about this or ideas about how you apply these habits and you'd like to share them" (5 Habits).
- **Never** "subscribe", "like" or "bell" (0 occurrences in 18.2k words).
- **A product feedback CTA in the big release:** "please let us know via the link to feedback in the left-hand side. I can't say that we'll change everything, but we will definitely read everything and consider what you're saying." (New Release 16:00).
- **Mid-video CTAs are cross-links:** "I will link to a video that goes into depth there" (Two-Way ~2:43); "there will be a video about this either coming out soon or already out depending on when I publish this." (Tasks/Projects ~9:05); "have a look at The next video" (Organize 9:06).
- **Sign-up and trial are in-flow, never a pitch:** "If you haven't started your free trial yet, it will prompt you to do that. just click this button and you can explore." (Getting Started ~1:12). "Sign up here" appears in only 3 descriptions.

### 2.7 Outros

No end card and no logo sting in any screencast. The last line is spoken over the app (Task Management ends on the real upload-date daily note, "December 9, 2025") or over the face-cam (Beth waves at 12:40 in Capture; Getting Started closes on a full-frame talking head, 9:25-9:38). The 16:38 release spends its last 50 s on an almost empty "Title" page while the thank-you plays.

### 2.9 Beat sheets by format

- **Feature launch** (Task Management, Two-Way, Variable Queries): why/problem (0-30 s), "in this video I'll show..." agenda, old way vs new way, setup, 3-4 worked examples ("Books & Author / People & Organization / People & Location / Goals & Projects"), the one gotcha ("the only thing that you need to know is that you must have multiple objects selected"), comment CTA. 4-12 min, 2-6 chapters.
- **Release walkthrough:** ritual greeting, "first things first" biggest feature, then features in descending size. Each gets what, how, which plan, limits, and sometimes "you may be wondering..." (5 occurrences, all in releases). Then quality-of-life items, "full list on our website", feedback and comment CTA. Hand-offs to engineers for their features (Luis on MCP, 5:35-11:39, including his own sign-off). 4-17 min.
- **Methodology:** the viewer's pain, the named framework or numbered list, "this is not a pipeline" reassurance, one step at a time (concept card, then the app or a personal example, then a rule of thumb), "a question I often get asked is...", recap, comment CTA. 8-23 min.
- **Getting started:** face intro and tagline, 3-idea mental model as graphics, captured items as the agenda, processing them one by one, built-in views, back to the start (bookend), the reassuring thesis ("it's not about bringing this ideal setup... It's just about bringing whatever is on your mind"), face outro.
- **Team chat / AMA** (from chapters and descriptions; no transcript): community questions read out and timestamped one per chapter (AMA: 25 chapters, description lists each question with its time range). Chats pair two team members on a method (GTD) and link the tutorial version.
- **Brand film:** 69 words, problem-agitation-resolution with an actor: "Ideas, they come to you out of nowhere... Organizing notes and ideas can be overwhelming. Okay, this is not working." then "Ideas and subjects, not folders. I like it." End card: logo + "A studio for your mind".

## 3. Voice and tone

- **Person:** "you/your" 46.5 per 1k words, "I/me/my" 37.2, "we/our" 11.0. "I" is the presenter's own practice ("I always send these things to my daily note and give them a tag", 5 Habits 7:12). "We" is the company speaking ("We want to keep things simple"; "We have basically gone through the entire app and made it calmer and simpler"). "You" carries the instructions.
- **Energy:** calm, warm, steady; a teacher, not a hype host. Excitement is stated, not performed: "This is another huge release and I'm excited to dig into it." Luis is more casual and ad-libbed: "Uh so", "Um", "Awesome.", "Perfect.", "It's looking very cool, I think."
- **Formality:** conversational and tidy. Beth's fillers are rare; self-corrections show she talks through an outline rather than reading a script: "I'll select click plus" (Getting Started ~0:57); "I've just manipulated the order a bit to to demonstrate this" (Tasks/Projects ~6:54). Release scripts are tighter: short declarative lines, one feature every 20-60 s.
- **Humor:** light, self-aware, everyday. "Capacities doesn't do files and folders. You've probably heard me say that a hundred times." (5 Habits 3:09). "at least in my case there's a bunch of typos" (1:49). "if I just send my daily note box of tissues, what does that actually mean?" (2:11). Demo tasks like "Find a nice cheese set to buy".
- **Signature phrases** (count in 18.2k words): "of course" 40, "really" 36, "simple/simply" 23, "powerful" 10, "built-in" 10, "out of my/your head" 5, "clarity" 5, "friction" 4 (all in one release). Positioning lines are repeated across videos: "the studio for your mind" (Getting Started; brand film; Shorts end card); "capacities is a note-taking app with tasks rather than a task manager" (Tasks/Projects ~6:00; echoed in the Short "Again, because Capacities is a note taking app..."); "contextualized task management" (Task Management ~5:18, ~11:18; also the thumbnail title); "there is no made up standard to maintain here" (Capture 1:34; "This is not a reasonable standard to maintain" at 9:35).
- **Limitations are handled plainly, in voice and in writing.** Spoken: "the only thing that you need to know is that you must have multiple objects selected. If you have a single object, you'll get this message..." (Two-Way ~5:51); "These are experimental and available for Capacities Pro." (New Release ~5:19); "So that is in beta testing for all believers... And after that it will come to the pro tier." (Release 70 2:12). Written: Getting Started's description: "Note - Reminders are not yet available. All other features shown in this video are available now." The Task Management description has a 6-question FAQ with blunt answers: "Can I add properties to the Capacities Task Object? This is not possible and it is not planned." / "Are recurring tasks supported? Not at this time." / "How can I work with subtasks? You can use checkboxes or tasks within tasks. There is no explicit concept for sub-tasks in Capacities."
- **Pricing and plans:** said once, early, without apology. "In order for this to work, you need to be on a paid plan. So, capacities pro or capacities believer." (Task Management ~0:31); "This is a feature available for people on the paid plans." (Release 70 2:26). Lower tiers get advice instead of a pitch: "If your on capacity is basic, you may also be wondering what you can do. And our advice is setting up automated exports." (2:58). Cost is transparent: "So in my time filming this morning, this is what I have used AI for and it's gone through a lot of my budget." (4:28). Upsells are soft: "you can get a larger AI budget and you don't have to do this long term. You could just use it for a month and see" (4:44). Plans also go into chapter names ("PDF Annotations (Believer)", 11 chapters carry a plan tag) and into "Features shown: Capacities Basic / Pro" lists (14 descriptions).
- **The cost of tiering:** "As a lowly Pro member, I'm finding it a little frustrating that I'm getting excited by the features in each of these videos that are understandably restricted to the elite tiers. But by the time they finally make it down to Pro, I've forgetten what I was excited about." (comment on the 2026-09 release).

## 4. On screen

- **Tooling.** 13/47 descriptions say "Filmed with Screen Studio" (2024-06 to 2026-03). What I see matches its defaults: pale gradient wallpaper, rounded window with a soft shadow, a webcam overlay that shrinks during zooms, and click-triggered zoom. Production is cheap and repeatable; the polish comes from the tool, not from an editor.
- **Face-cam:**
  - 2024-2025: a rounded square bottom-right, 15-20% of frame width (Web Extension and Organize are the biggest).
  - 2026: a circle bottom-right, ~12% of width, with a small Capacities logo badge on its rim (Getting Started, Tasks/Projects, both 2026-09 releases).
  - It is present in 95-100% of screencast frames (40/40 in Task Management, Release 70 and Tasks/Projects). On zooms it shrinks to 6-8% or disappears (Two-Way 0:30; Web Extension 0:29; Task Management 6:40), and it hides behind full-screen cards (Capture 0:15-0:40).
  - Full-frame talking head appears only in Getting Started (0:00-0:05 and 0:35, then 9:25-9:38), the whole of 5 Core Habits, and Luis's spliced sign-off in the 2026-09 release (11:30).
  - The second presenter keeps the same circle; his segment is also the only dark-theme footage (6:00-6:50).
- **Framing.** The app window fills ~92-95% of frame width. Wallpaper: pale blue (2024-2025), warm peach (Dec 2025), cream/yellow (2026). Light theme everywhere except Luis's segment.
- **Zoom has faded over time:**
